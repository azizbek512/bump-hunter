Hunting the Higgs Boson ($H \to \gamma\gamma$): Profile Likelihoods, $5\sigma$ Discovery & Look-Elsewhere Effect

In High Energy Physics at the CERN Large Hadron Collider (ATLAS & CMS), new particles are not observed directly as isolated photographs. Poisson Negative Log-Likelihood ($\text{NLL}$) & Single-Bin Penalty Intuition

Why $\nu - n \ln \nu$?

Each histogram bin $i$ is an independent Poisson counter with expected yield $\nu_i(\mu) = \mu S_i + B_i$ and observed count $n_i$. Dropping the constant $\ln(n_i!)$, the Negative Log-Likelihood penalty is:

NLL(ν) = Σ [ νᵢ − nᵢ · ln(νᵢ) ]

Setting $\frac{\partial \text{NLL}}{\partial \nu} = 1 - \frac{n}{\nu} = 0$ proves that the penalty is strictly minimized when the model expectation equals the observed data ($\nu = n$).

One-Sided Test Statistic $q_0$

To test whether the collider data in Station 01 contains a positive signal excess ($\hat{\mu} > 0$) against the background-only null hypothesis ($\mu = 0$), we compute:

q₀ = 2 · (NLL₀ − NLL₁) if μ̂ > 0 else 0

Under Wilks' asymptotic theorem, significance in Gaussian standard deviations is simply $Z = \sqrt{q_0}$, with local $p$-value $p_{\text{local}} = 1 - \Phi(Z)$.

Mass Scan ($110\text{--}150\text{ GeV}$), Look-Elsewhere Effect & $95\%$ CL Upper Limits

1. Look-Elsewhere Effect (LEE)

When searching across $N_{\text{trials}}$ mass hypotheses without knowing $m_H$ in advance, the probability of a random background fluctuation anywhere in the search window increases:

p_global = 1 − (1 − p_local)^(N_trials)
2. 95% CL Upper Limit ($\mu_{95}$)

Where no signal is observed, physicists set a $95\%$ confidence upper bound $\mu_{95} = \max(0, \hat{\mu}) + 1.645\sigma_\mu$, which tightens as $\sigma_\mu \propto 1/\sqrt{\mathcal{L}}$ with higher collider luminosity.
